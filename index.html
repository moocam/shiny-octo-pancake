Tap a chord to hear it

Power chords

The engine of punk. Two or three notes, the same shape anywhere on the neck. The red dot is the root, which gives the chord its name. Use the first finger on the root and the third and fourth fingers two frets up.

Open chords

For strumming and the quieter bits. An x means don't play that string. An o means play it open.

Jam tracks

Play lead over a band

Pick a track, press Play, and improvise with the notes on the fretboard. Start slow. There are no wrong notes in the highlighted scale, only better and worse ones.

Song chords are written from memory and simplified, so check them against a tab site before relying on them. Tempos are approximate. The backing is in standard tuning, so you never need to retune.

Reference notes

Tuning by ear instead

  1. Tap a string's button under Reference notes, then play that string on the guitar.
  2. Turn the tuning peg slowly. If the two notes wobble (wah-wah-wah), you're close. The wobble slows down as you get nearer.
  3. When the wobble stops, it's in tune. Do all six, then check them again, because tuning one string pulls the others slightly.

The speed rule

Find the speed where you can play an exercise perfectly five times in a row. Then go up 5 BPM. If it falls apart, go back down. Clean and slow beats fast and messy every time.
